Why people pick up the phone.

Nobody rings to declare that rodents have arrived. They describe a noise, an odor or a mess, and those descriptions help us most.

  • Heavy thumping over the ceiling near dusk.
  • Shredded nest scraps in a vent, an engine bay or a storage tub.
  • Oily smudges along walls and pipes.
  • A dog or cat obsessed with a single corner.
  • Droppings that come back after you clean.

From walk-through to check-back.

A smell with no visible source calls for odor and dead rodent removal, which follows the odor to a wall, ceiling or crawlspace before anything is opened. If the walk-through turns up less trouble than you feared, we say so.

Houses used only part of the year.

Before you leave, empty the pantry of open food, close vents with mesh and check the garage door seal. When you return, look at counters, drawers and the attic hatch before settling in. Our blog has a full checklist for empty houses.

If you return and find droppings, do not sweep them. Open windows, air out the house, put on gloves and use the wet method from the CDC pages linked below. Then phone us and we will trace the route.

Why timing counts here.

Autumn is a common time to first hear scratching. Cold nights push mice and rats to look for shelter, and an opening missed in summer turns into the way in. Early fall is a fine time to check vents, garage corners and crawlspace screens.

What decides the first visit.

A house that sits empty for part of the year is a common reason for a call, in any town. If yours is used seasonally, tell us. A closed-up house with food in the pantry and dim corners can host animals for weeks before anyone notices.

Vehicles and stored equipment are easy to forget. A truck, a boat trailer or a stack of totes can shelter animals for weeks, and the nest may be in an engine bay or under a cover and not in the house.

Before we arrive: cleaning rodent waste safely

Agencies agree on the main points of cleanup, so those shared points are gathered here in brief.

  • Whatever the mess looks like, resist dry methods and start with water or disinfectant.
  • A garage, shed or crawlspace that has been shut for a while needs roughly thirty minutes of outside air before you enter.
  • Rubber or vinyl gloves go on for anything stained or soiled, and your hands get a long wash in warm soapy water afterward.
  • Sequence counts: wet it, rest it for five minutes or so, then wipe, which keeps dust down while the product acts.
  • Knot the bag hard before it leaves the house, and keep your hands covered if a dead rodent must be moved.
  • If fever or short breath appears, phone your own doctor and open with the exposure.

Where the safety advice comes from

Instead of asking you to take our word, we name the full pages where the guidance began.

  • Cleaning Up After Rodents, from the CDC: Airing out a closed space, wetting droppings rather than sweeping, and bagging waste with care.
