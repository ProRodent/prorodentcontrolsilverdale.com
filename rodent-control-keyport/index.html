Safety steps for waste and nests

Meant for homeowners, the list keeps dust low while a technician is still on the way.

  • No broom. Sweeping dry pellets throws fine particles into the very air you breathe.
  • Closed-up rooms hold stale air, so open the doors and windows and stay out for about half an hour.
  • Start by protecting your hands and finish with a thorough scrub.
  • Think of three beats, soak then pause then wipe, where the pause lasts around five minutes.
  • Bag, knot, then bin. A carcass is never handled unprotected.
  • Fever, aches or breathing trouble in the following weeks call for a doctor, who should hear that rodent waste was close by. Our trade is pests, not medicine.

Where the safety advice comes from

Our advice about cleanup and about poison draws on two federal sources, and both appear below.

  • Cleaning Up After Rodents, from the CDC: Airing out a closed space, wetting droppings rather than sweeping, and bagging waste with care.
  • Rodenticide Restrictions, from the EPA: Consumer rodenticide rules, helpful if you are weighing a store-bought product.
