How we read a Silverdale property.

Silverdale is an unincorporated community in Kitsap County, set at the north tip of Dyes Inlet, roughly nine miles northwest of Bremerton and about nine miles south of Poulsbo. It has no mayor or city hall of its own, which is worth knowing when you wonder who handles what. For a rodent job, the building in front of us matters more than the map.

Wet weather lasts a long time across the Pacific Northwest, and lots with tall conifers, ferns and dense brush are common. Where a house stands among those, limbs on the roof, shrubs against the siding and firewood on a wall can all give animals a route and a hiding place. Ten minutes outside with a flashlight will show which of these fit your place.

Mouse, rat or something else?

House mice slip through a gap the width of a pencil. Norway and roof rats want about half an inch, then climb along fences, cables and limbs toward the eaves. Deer mice lean toward wood edges, sheds and garage corners.

Which animal it is determines the trap, the sealing material and the cleanup method. A few words by phone get us started, and the walk-through supplies the remainder. Naming the animal falls to us.

When the rain settles in.

Rodents mostly travel in the dark, so evidence tends to show up at night. Long wet spells can push animals toward dry, warm cover, and a heated house fits the description. Tell us the room where the noise came from and roughly when it started.

  • At the start of every wet season, look over vents, pipe collars and the corners of the garage door.
  • Keep kibble, wild bird mix and lawn seed in tubs with sealing lids.
  • Stack firewood and lumber on a rack, away from the walls.
  • Cut back any limb or vine that reaches the roof or leans on the siding.

Before a technician arrives.

Four quick moves protect everyone at home and preserve the evidence. Leave droppings unswept and unvacuumed, keep kids and pets away from that room, and let traps already set remain where they sit.

  • Take photos of droppings, tooth marks or damage, placing a coin beside each to show scale.
  • Note the room and the clock time for every noise.
  • Shut the door to the room showing the most sign.
  • Skip loose bait and sticky boards, which bring in fresh hazards.

    Greasy dark smudges on walls, pipes and joists trace a path an animal takes each night.

  • A smell.

    Sharp ammonia-like odor in a closed room, or a heavier reek wherever an animal has died.

Answers for Silverdale households.

How can I tell a mouse from a rat?

Look at size and position. Tiny pellets in drawers and cabinets hint at mice, while bigger ones along a roof edge or spread across an attic hint at rats. Burrows by a foundation or beneath a shed lean toward rats too.

Where do rodents enter a house?

Mice pass through gaps as slim as a pencil, and rats need about double. Usual entries include spaces around pipes and wires, vents, the corners of a garage door, crawlspace screens and the roof edge.

Is trapping enough on its own?

Traps take out animals already indoors, and sealing keeps new ones from coming in. Most jobs call for both in that order, followed by a return visit to confirm the work held.

Will the work be safe around children and pets?

Let us know who lives in the home before we begin. We place traps and stations out of reach and then report what went where.

How fast can someone come?

The phone is answered day and night, and a same-day visit is possible when there is room on the schedule. Describe your find and ask what is open.

Is Silverdale a city?

No. Silverdale is an unincorporated community, also a census-designated place, in Kitsap County, so there is no city hall. We cover it by ZIP code, 98315 and 98383, and by the street you state on the call.
