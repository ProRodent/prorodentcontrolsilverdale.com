Before we arrive: cleaning rodent waste safely

Going slowly at the outset keeps you from breathing in anything airborne and unpleasant.

  • Put away the compressed air, the broom and the dry rag whenever you find pellets or stained nesting.
  • Closed-up rooms hold stale air, so open the doors and windows and stay out for about half an hour.
  • Either disposable or reusable rubber gloves will do, as long as you wash both the gloves and your hands.
  • Use a sprayer until nothing looks dry, and allow around five minutes before you wipe.
  • Knot the bag hard before it leaves the house, and keep your hands covered if a dead rodent must be moved.
  • Flu-like symptoms after a cleanup are worth mentioning to a physician, because diagnosis is not what we do.
