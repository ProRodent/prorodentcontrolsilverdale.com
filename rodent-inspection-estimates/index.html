Why looking comes first.

A price quoted over the phone for an unseen problem is a guess. Rodent work depends on the animal, the building and the routes between them, and none of those can be known from a description. The walk-through replaces guesswork with a list of facts.

The visit also keeps you from paying for the wrong job. Noises overhead might be rats, squirrels or a loose duct, and each calls for a different fix. Learning which one before spending anything is the whole point of this visit.

What the technician examines.

The route usually starts outside. The roofline, vents, soffits, foundation and crawlspace screens are walked first, because that is where animals enter. Then the attic, the garage, any outbuildings and the spaces under sinks are checked from the inside.

  • Roof edge, soffit and fascia joins for gaps and stains.
  • Vent covers, pipe sleeves and utility entries.
  • Attic insulation for tunnels, nests and droppings.
  • Crawlspace or basement for gnawed ducts and burrows near the foundation.
  • Garage door seals and the wall base where totes are stacked.

Local ground, local questions.

Silverdale is an unincorporated Kitsap County community at the north tip of Dyes Inlet, and every inspection begins with your own building. Your property might be an open lot, a stand of tall conifers or a spot near the retail areas around Kitsap Mall, and each setting suggests different questions about cover, drainage and shared waste.

We ask about those details on the call, and you do not need exact answers. If your property is near Silverdale Waterfront Park, Clear Creek Trail or a commercial area, simply say so and the visit is planned around it.

After the visit.

You leave with the findings explained, a plan with prices, and a clear idea of what can wait. Nothing is booked on the spot. Many people take a day to decide, and that is fine.

If the plan includes trapping, trapping and removal follows, and sealing comes after the catches slow. The order is chosen so that nothing is closed in behind a wall.

Worth saying on the phone.

  • Where and when you notice sound or sign.
  • Whether the house sits over a slab, crawlspace or basement.
  • Attic access and its location.
  • Pets, children or vehicles that could be affected.

Prepare for the visit.

  • A car with nesting material under the hood stays parked until it has been checked.
  • Carry lawn seed, pet kibble and birdseed into the house for now.
  • Keep any chewed object in a bag for the technician.
  • Know the location of the main electrical panel, since a wire may have been gnawed.

What to do while you wait, and what to skip

Read every line before you touch a thing, because one minute of reading protects your lungs.

  • Put away the compressed air, the broom and the dry rag whenever you find pellets or stained nesting.
  • Stale air can carry what the eye misses. Open up, back away and wait half an hour.
  • Germs are too small to see, so gloves go on before you start and soap and water finish the job.
  • Dampen each pellet and stain with disinfectant and let it stand about five minutes before picking anything up.
  • Seal the bag, and keep skin away from the animal.
  • A doctor alone can judge the seriousness of an exposure, so call one if you develop fever or labored breathing.

What you take away from an inspection.

  • The animal identified.

    Mouse, rat or something else, based on droppings, tracks, tooth marks and the routes the sign follows.

  • A map of entries.

    Each opening in use is noted and marked on a plan, with photographs where they help.

  • A plan with prices.

    Whether it is trapping, sealing, cleanup or repair, each job is quoted by itself, so you choose which to book and when.

Questions about the walk-through.

How long does an inspection take?

That varies with the size of the property and how many spaces can be reached. The technician will go through the attic, then the crawlspace or basement, then the garage, the yard and the roof edge.

Must I be at home?

Someone needs to be there to let us in and to hear the findings. Mention gates, dogs and anything locked.

Do I get a price at the end?

The plan and price come when the technician knows the scope. Sealing, cleanup and repair are priced apart, which keeps each cost visible.

What happens if nothing turns up?

Then we say so plainly. A sound in the walls can come from pipes, wind or other animals, and an honest negative saves you money.

Can I book one just for a rental or a purchase?

Yes. An inspection works for a property you are about to rent out, move into or buy, and the findings are explained in plain language.
