How mice settle into a Silverdale home.

No rodent settles into a house as thoroughly as the house mouse. It nests inside wall cavities, beneath appliances, in boxes of seasonal gear and in attic insulation, usually with food close at hand. One pair can grow into a crowd in a few months.

Clues here are tiny. Look for droppings shaped like dark rice grains, fine tooth marks on wrappers and soft plastic, and scratching in a wall that may run on a while before stopping. Because mice are mostly nocturnal, plenty of homeowners learn of them only when the house goes quiet.

Silverdale sits at the north tip of Dyes Inlet, and a lot may back onto woods, brush or open green ground. If your home is close to Clear Creek Trail or Silverdale Waterfront Park, mention it, since a lot beside open ground may have more cover to check along that side.

Following the route before a trap goes down.

Work starts where sign is heaviest and spreads outward. Fresh droppings, greasy smears and nibbled corners outline a route, and each route ends at an entry. Trapping with no map takes out a few animals and leaves the cause alone.

  • Look under the sinks, around and behind the stove and fridge, and in the closet holding the water heater.
  • Look at garage door corners, the rubber seal along the bottom and the seam where wall meets slab.
  • Examine the attic hatch, vent edges and each place where plumbing or wiring crosses a wall.
  • Check crawlspace screens and foundation vents on the side of the house that stays wettest.

Catch the animals, then close the openings.

We set snap traps and enclosed stations along the evidence, away from kids and pets. Mice follow wall lines and investigate anything new, so a trap flush with a baseboard does better than one in open floor.

Sealing begins after the first catches show which animals are active. Steel wool used alone fails, because mice pull it out. Each opening gets flashing, mesh and sealant, and you can read about the materials on the exclusion page.

Small things to do while you wait.

Keep brooms away from dry droppings and keep loose bait out of the house, since sweeping raises dust. Planning to clean before we arrive? Use the wet method described on the CDC pages linked below.

Storage counts in a damp climate. Dry goods and pet food go in sealed tubs, and food and water bowls come up for the night. This will not solve the problem alone, but it takes away the payoff that keeps luring mice back.

After the traps have finished.

Success looks like quiet nights, no fresh droppings and empty traps. A heavily soiled attic or wall cavity calls for attic and crawlspace cleanup, and chewed cables or insulation go to rodent damage repair.

Not certain whether mice or something larger is responsible? Start with an inspection, after which you know the animal and hold a plan.

  • Shut the door on the worst room and keep it shut.

  • Jot down the hour the noises begin, since the clock hints at the route.

A safety checklist for droppings

Going slowly at the outset keeps you from breathing in anything airborne and unpleasant.

  • No broom. Sweeping dry pellets throws fine particles into the very air you breathe.
  • A garage, shed or crawlspace that has been shut for a while needs roughly thirty minutes of outside air before you enter.
  • Gloves go on at the start, and handwashing closes the job.
  • Think of three beats, soak then pause then wipe, where the pause lasts around five minutes.
  • Use a bag that seals rather than an open bin, and put gloves or a tool between you and any carcass.
  • Body aches, a fever or labored breath after contact with waste are matters for a clinic and not a pest company.

Questions Silverdale callers ask about mice.

What size of hole can a mouse squeeze through?

Picture a pencil pushed into drywall, and you have the width in question. It could be the space around a drain pipe, behind a dryer vent, next to a door corner or around a cable entering a wall.

Do mice come inside when the weather turns wet and cold?

Often, yes. A run of rainy weather and cool nights sends small animals in search of dry warmth, and a heated house fits. A gap overlooked all summer can turn into a favorite door by November.

May I set out poison from the store in the meantime?

Store bait reaches homes as loaded stations, and a mouse that feeds at one may crawl into a wall and die there. Talk to the technician before you use any.

How soon will the scratching end?

It hinges on the number of mice and on whether every entry gets closed. A realistic timeline comes after the walk-through.

Are mouse calls answered overnight?

Yes. A person answers at any hour, and a technician can arrive the same day when the schedule has space.
