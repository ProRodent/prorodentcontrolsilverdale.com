Shared walls, lanes and back doors.

Property boundaries are invisible to animals. One mouse trapped from a building may turn up next door by way of a common wall, a pipe chase or a gap beside a service door. If a wall, cellar or service lane is shared, involve the neighbor or landlord, because only a fully sealed structure holds.

  • Keep rear doors shut except for deliveries and trash runs.
  • Seal gaps where pipes and wires cross a shared wall.
  • Pick bins whose lids fit snugly, and rinse them from time to time.
  • Agree on a single pickup schedule so bags are not left out overnight.
Where buildings crowd together, the weakest wall decides how the job turns out.

Houses and shops sharing a block.

Do you operate a cafe, shop or studio? On the commercial rodent control page you will find scheduled checks and written notes described. Do you live over or beside a business? Ask how its waste is handled, since one shared dumpster feeds the whole lane.

An inspection can start with either side of a shared wall, and the report notes what the building as a whole needs. Where a neighbor is involved, we suggest ways to approach them without blame.

Cars, vans and delivery vehicles.

A vehicle left for days, in a lane or a small lot, can turn into a nest site. Open the hood and check the cabin vents after any long absence, and see the vehicle page for details. If you notice nest material, leave the engine off.

Wet-season habits in a commercial area.

With the rain settling in and temperatures dropping, animals look for warmth and dry cover, and a heated storeroom or basement close to the bins is a prime target. Before the wet season, walk the rear of the building, test door sweeps and screens, then test them again after the first storm.

Found droppings? Use the wet cleanup steps from the CDC pages linked below and leave the broom in the closet. Then call, so the route can be traced before another batch appears.

What to tell the dispatcher.

Give us the street or the closest cross street, tell us if the place is a shop, a home or both, and mention any shared wall or service lane. Add any basement or crawlspace, and tell us if food is stored or served there. That sends the first visit to the right spot.

Prepare for the visit.

  • After dinner, put pet food and birdseed in tubs with lids.
  • Lift water bowls at night and wipe down the counters.
  • Hold back on home trapping so the activity pattern stays legible.
  • Recall the date of the very first noise or odor and put it on paper.

Before you sweep, spray or bag: safety steps

Read every line before you touch a thing, because one minute of reading protects your lungs.

  • Pellets can look harmless and still crumble into powder, and powder is what gets inhaled, so leave it unswept.
  • A sealed outbuilding should not be entered cold, so let a breeze run through for about thirty minutes.
  • Bare hands never touch the area, from the first minute to the last.
  • Saturate the pellets and the spot beneath them with household disinfectant, or bleach diluted as the label says. Leave it about five minutes. Then wipe everything up with paper towels.
  • All that you wiped up gets sealed in a bag before it reaches a bin, and a dead animal calls for gloves.
  • Body aches, a fever or labored breath after contact with waste are matters for a clinic and not a pest company.

Old Town, answered.

Is Old Town Silverdale a real name?

Yes. Public sources use the name for one of Silverdale's two commercial areas. We could not confirm a boundary, so phone with your address, and we will tell you in plain words if it is covered.

Do older buildings draw more rodents?

Age by itself is not the cause. An older structure can have more joints and unsealed utility holes, which makes inspection slower, and a newer one may have gaps too.

Which ZIP code is Old Town in?

Silverdale has two ZIP codes in our data, 98315 and 98383. If your mail shows a different one, a short call lets us check the address.

Do you work with a neighbor or landlord?

Yes. We can outline sealing that would cover the whole building, and the cost can then be split. We enter only with the owner's agreement.
