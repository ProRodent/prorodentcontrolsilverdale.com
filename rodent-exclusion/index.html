Why sealing is the lasting half of the job.

Trapping handles the animals that are inside now. Sealing prevents the next group from arriving. Without it, a quiet month can end with fresh pellets as new animals find the same gap, and the cycle starts again.

The work is slower than people expect, because every opening matters. A mouse needs a quarter inch, and a house has dozens of places that size. The technician walks the whole envelope and notes each one.

Where houses leak to rodents.

Gaps hide at transitions: where a pipe crosses a wall, where a vent meets siding, where a garage door meets the slab. Wet-climate houses add rot at the base of trim and soft spots near gutters, which turn into entries over time.

  • Soffit and fascia joins that have shrunk or rotted.
  • Gaps around pipes, cables and meter bases.
  • Dryer, bath and range vent covers without a screen.
  • Foundation vents with torn mesh.
  • Garage door bottoms and side seals.
  • Spaces behind chimney flashing and under roof edges.

Materials that work.

The kit for exclusion is plain. Galvanized hardware cloth blocks gaps and vents, sheet metal covers damaged edges, and copper mesh or metal wool packs small holes beneath sealant. None of these can be chewed away.

Wooded lots and wet seasons.

On a wooded lot, long damp spells can soften wood near gutters, decks and trim. Those spots are checked because softened wood is easy to gnaw. Where a limb touches the roof, trimming it helps, and we point out the ones to cut.

If your home is near Clear Creek Trail or the shore by Silverdale Waterfront Park, ask the technician to start with the side facing it. Open green ground can feed a steady trickle of animals toward the nearest house.

What to expect on the day.

The work follows the survey and trapping. A crew closes the openings in order, starting with the largest and the most used. Where a gap sits high on the house, a ladder or lift may be needed, and we say so in advance.

When sealing wraps up, a return check confirms nothing was overlooked. If animals remain inside, trapping continues until they are out. Cleanup and repairs follow when the entries are shut.

Good to have in hand.

  • Siding type and any trim that looks warped or soft.
  • Vent covers you know are missing or loose.
  • Garage door brand or age, if you know it.
  • Spots where pipes, wires or cables enter the house.

Before anyone arrives.

  • Shut the door on the worst room and keep it shut.
  • Dry pellets stay where they lie, because lifting them dry is the hazard.
  • No hardware store bait for now: an animal that eats it may die inside a wall.
  • Jot down the hour the noises begin, since the clock hints at the route.

Safety guidance from the CDC cleanup page

Rodent waste can carry germs, so slow and careful beats quick. Each point follows the CDC cleanup guidance named at the bottom of the page.

  • No broom. Sweeping dry pellets throws fine particles into the very air you breathe.
  • Air the space first. Open whatever opens, then wait about thirty minutes.
  • Nearby surfaces may hold germs too small to see, so pair gloves with a proper wash.
  • Disinfect before collecting. The wet waste should sit around five minutes first.
  • A knotted bag goes in the household trash, and a dead rodent should never be picked up bare handed.
  • If a flu-like illness or shortness of breath follows exposure, a physician should hear about the cleanup and make the diagnosis.

Sealing questions, answered.

Why not just caulk the holes myself?

Caulk and foam do not stop teeth, and plugging a hole with an animal inside can send it deeper into the house. Wait until the technician confirms the animals have left.

How small must a gap be to stop mice?

Close anything wider than about a quarter inch for mice or half an inch for rats, using material that cannot be chewed.

Does sealing hurt the house?

Not when done well. Flashing, mesh and sealant are installed to match the siding and trim, and ventilation openings stay open behind screens.

How long does it last?

Metal and mesh can last for years, but wet climates and shifting wood can open new gaps. A yearly walk around the outside catches most of them.

Will you seal the roof too?

The roofline and its junctions are checked. Where work requires a roofer or a carpenter, we say so and explain what needs fixing.
