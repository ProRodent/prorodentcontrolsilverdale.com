The order we work in.

A job runs in four steps, and the order matters. First comes the survey of attic, crawlspace or basement, garage, yard and roofline. Then animals are trapped out, then the openings are closed, and last come cleanup and any repair.

  • Survey the property and name the animal.
  • Trap and remove what is inside.
  • Seal every opening with metal, mesh or sturdy sealant.
  • Clean, repair and return to confirm the result.

Skipping ahead causes trouble. Sealing before the animals are out can trap them in a wall, and cleaning before sealing invites a fresh mess. A price for each stage is given before that stage begins.

Where poison fits, and where it does not.

Rodent poison is regulated. The EPA page listed under sources explains what homeowners can buy and why. Our approach leans on traps and sealing, and if any product goes out, you hear what it is and where it was put.

Cleanup follows the CDC method of soaking, wiping and bagging. We handle pests, not people, so questions about anyone's health belong with a doctor.

What we will not promise.

No one can count animals in a wall without looking, and no one can promise a rodent will never return to a wooded Kitsap lot. We do promise a careful search for entries, lasting materials and a follow-up check.

How quickly things move.

A same-day visit is offered whenever the day has room, and the first available window is quoted on the call. Where a place lies across water or in another county, we tell you honestly when the earliest trip can happen, and we group tasks to make the most of it.
